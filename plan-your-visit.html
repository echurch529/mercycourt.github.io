Welcome

We're Glad You're Here

We believe church is more than a building you walk into. It is a family you can belong to, a place where you can grow, serve, worship, and discover the life God has called you to live.

At Mercy Court, you will find people from different walks of life coming together with one heart: to experience God's mercy, grow in faith, and become Christlike pacesetters in our world. Whether you are new to church, returning after a long time, searching for community, or looking for a place where your family can grow spiritually, we would love to welcome you.

Come as you are. There is a place for you here.

Your First Visit

What to Expect When You Visit

Visiting a church for the first time can feel like a big step, so we want to make it simple and comfortable for you. From the moment you arrive, our goal is to make you feel welcomed, valued, and at home.

You do not have to know all the songs. You do not have to have everything figured out. You do not have to look a certain way. Just come with an open heart, and we will be glad to receive you.

At Mercy Court, you can expect passionate worship, practical Bible teaching, a loving church family, and a peaceful environment where you and your family can connect with God and people.

FAQs

Questions Before You Visit?

We understand that getting to church is not always easy, and we do not want transportation to be the reason you miss the opportunity to worship with us. If you are planning to visit Mercy Court and need help with transportation, please let us know ahead of time. Our team will do our best to connect with you, understand your need, and provide available guidance or support where possible. To request transportation assistance, please call Mercy Court's contact line before your planned visit.

Wear what makes you comfortable and respectful for worship. Some people dress formally, while others come in more casual clothing. You do not need to dress a certain way to belong here. We are more excited to meet you than to inspect what you are wearing.

At Mercy Court, we believe church is not only a place of worship, but also a place where God's love is expressed through care and practical support. Through our Food Pantry, we serve individuals and families who may need food assistance. Whether you are a member, a visitor, or someone in the community looking for support, we would love to share more about how this ministry serves people with compassion and dignity.

Our worship is joyful, heartfelt, and Spirit-filled. You may hear contemporary worship, traditional gospel sounds, African praise, hymns, and songs that reflect our diverse church family. Our goal is simple: to create an atmosphere where people can genuinely worship God.

Our Sunday services usually last about two hours. During that time, we worship, pray, hear announcements, receive teaching from God's Word, and connect as a church family. Some Sundays may be slightly longer during special programs or celebrations.

Yes, depending on the Sunday or event, refreshments may be available after service. More importantly, there is always room for conversation, connection, and fellowship. We encourage visitors to stay for a few minutes after service so we can meet you properly.

Our children's ministry is designed to help children learn about God in a safe, joyful, and age-appropriate environment. We want children to love church, understand God's Word, build godly friendships, and grow with confidence in who God created them to be.

No. You are welcome even if you are exploring faith, asking questions, or simply looking for community. Mercy Court is a place where people can encounter God, grow at their own pace, and find support for life's journey.

We love welcoming new people, but we also want you to feel comfortable. Depending on the service, visitors may be acknowledged warmly, but you will not be pressured or embarrassed. Our goal is to honor you, not put you on the spot.

Yes. You can connect with us through our online platforms and YouTube channel. Our E-Church experience exists to help online worshipers stay engaged and connected, whether they are watching from home or preparing to visit in person.

Families

A Place for Families to Grow Together

At Mercy Court, we believe families are important to God and central to a healthy church community. Whether you are raising young children, parenting teenagers, building a marriage, or seeking a stronger spiritual foundation for your home, we want to walk with you.

Our church is a place where families can worship together, grow together, serve together, and build lasting relationships with other families. We want every family that walks through our doors to feel supported, strengthened, and encouraged.

From children's ministry to youth engagement, family-centered events, prayer, and practical teaching, Mercy Court is committed to helping homes flourish.

Children's Ministry

A Joyful Place for Children to Know God

We believe children are not just the future of the church. They are a vital part of the church today.

Our children's ministry creates a safe, welcoming, and joyful environment where children can learn about God, build friendships, and develop a strong foundation of faith. Through age-appropriate teaching, engaging activities, prayer, and loving guidance, we help children understand that they are known, loved, and created for purpose.

When you visit Mercy Court, your children will be received with care, patience, and joy.

Youth

A Place for Youth to Belong, Grow, and Lead

We believe young people need more than activities. They need identity, guidance, friendship, purpose, and a safe place to ask real questions.

At Mercy Court, our youth are encouraged to grow in faith, develop godly character, build meaningful friendships, and discover their gifts. We want them to know that church is not just for adults. It is a place where they can be seen, heard, equipped, and inspired to become the pacesetters God has called them to be.

Whether they are navigating school, friendships, identity, purpose, or the pressures of today's world, we want our youth to know they do not have to walk alone.
